# After 1.14: where Zimi goes next

Written overnight 2026-10-12 at Eric's ask ("plan our future"), from the plans in this folder, the feature inventory, the issue history and tonight's reviews. A proposal to decide on, not a decree.

## Where things stand

- Shipping fast: 1.11.0 (09-27), 1.12.0 (09-30), 1.13.0 (10-06), 1.13.1 (10-09), 1.13.2 (10-11), 1.14 today. One open issue (#53, which 1.14 closes), no open PRs.
- One tester carries the backlog: tripplehelix filed 32 of the last 40 issues. They are Zimi's QA department, and tonight's goodwill reply matters more than its length suggests.
- Zimi has 7+ apps, 10 languages, an MCP package, Create, BitTorrent with three engines, and 83 GitHub stars. The product has outgrown its audience.
- Tonight four independent reviewers found 30+ real defects in 1.14 before it shipped (a stranger keeping admin after the password was turned off; a client's seeding rule deleting library ZIMs; a switch deleting a finished download). Every one is fixed with a test that fails on the old code. That review step is the single best quality lever this project has found.

### 1.16: The apps, shared and in every language

Needs a hero. Eric, 2026-10-12: "Shareable links also doesn't feel like a hero feature." Everything below is supporting work; the hero is still open.

- **Shareable links everywhere** (Eric, 2026-10-12: "useful shareable links all around"): every view a person might send someone is a URL that opens the same view. The Maps place and zoom, an Almanac date and view, an app's filter or open item (a Tube talk at a time, an Exchange question, a Bookshelf author, a Dictionary word), search with its chips. ## Park (with reasons)

- **Satellite passes over you** (from God's Eye View): Eric, 2026-10-12: "Nah to the satellite thing."

## How we work (from tonight)

- **Independent review before every release:** three read-only reviewers (engine, everything else, frontend) on `main...<branch>`, plus a fourth for the next release's research. Findings go into an overnight-style tracking doc; each fix gets a test proven to fail on the old code.
- **A slower, themed cadence:** one release a week with a theme beats one every two days. The inventory grows faster than it ships; themes force the trade-offs.
- **A person-waiting sweep each release:** every issue or thread where someone waits gets a reply in the same release (L3tum waited two months on #53).
- **Drive Eric's path:** NAS first, 390 px phone pass, real end to end (tonight: a real download through qBittorrent behind the VPN).
