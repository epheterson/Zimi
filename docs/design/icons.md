# Making a Zimi icon

Everything here is reproduced from numbers by `python3 scripts/icons/build.py` (run from the repo root); read this page first, then run it, then change one thing at a time.

## What the script does

`build.py` writes `mark.svg`, `zimigames.svg`, `favicon.svg` and `almanac.svg` into `--out DIR` (default a temp dir), and the output is byte-identical from run to run. `--apply` rewrites `zimi/builtin/games/mark.svg` and the generated `ZMARK` block in `zimi/builtin/games/cards.js`; `--icons` rasterises both ZIM icons to 96x96 RGBA PNGs with transparent corners through headless Chrome and ImageMagick; `--check` fails if the repo drifted from the generator; `--review` writes a board of both icons at 1024, 96, 48 and 32. After `--apply` or `--icons` the own ZIMs need rebuilding (`python3 -m zimi.ownzims`, and `--check` must be clean). The helpers live beside it: `astro.py` (Sun and Moon), `glitter.py` (sea glitter), `splat.py` (the smooth closed curve), `zglyph.py` (the Z from the font) and `findfont.py` (how the font was identified).

## The mark

The mark is our Z on a river pebble, and the Z is rendered from the font, never traced. It is SF Compact at its default instance (wght 1000, Black), glyph "Z", straight from `/System/Library/Fonts/SFCompact.ttf`; it is the same Z as `zimi/assets/icon.png`, and it is not SF Pro Bold, which Eric called the thin one. `python3 scripts/icons/zglyph.py` extracts the outline with fontTools and checks it against `ZG` in `build.py`. The outline is twelve points on a 2048 em with y up: (110,0) (1184,0) (1184,326) (611,326) (611,346) (1161,1103) (1161,1365) (115,1365) (115,1039) (660,1039) (660,1020) (110,262). The coordinates are fixed because they are the font's own and the app icon already ships this glyph; changing one by a unit makes the family drift, so a different look means a different decision, not an edit to `ZG`. The Z is set the way the app icon sets it: font size 0.72 of its box, glyph bounds centred, box `ZB` = 31 units on the 100-unit icon, centred on the pebble.

The pebble is a smooth, convex, slightly worn river stone, centre (19.4, 18.2) on the 100-unit icon, radii 15.0 by 12.6, tilted -8 degrees. It is an ellipse radius scaled by k(t) = 1 + 0.028 cos(2t + 0.9) + 0.014 sin(3t + 0.3) - 0.012 cos(t - 0.6), sampled at 40 points, with the underside flattened by 0.6 times max(0, sin t) cubed, and joined by a closed Catmull-Rom spline (`splat.catmull`). Only low harmonics, so it stays convex and never reads as a blob or a shape. The Z's gradient runs corner to corner of the Z's box through #f59e0b, #f97316 (at half) and #ef4444. The stone's fill runs top to bottom through #1f1a17, #0d0b0a (at 55 percent) and #070605, which is a warm near-black so it reads as a stone and not a sticker. Under it is a soft drop shadow (dx 0, dy 1.6, blur 1.6, black at 0.45), and around it a faint edge, a white stroke 0.6 wide at 0.10 opacity. The mark alone is `mark.svg`, viewBox `2 2 36 33`; it holds at 22 to 24 px with its shadow, so there is no flatter small variant.

## Where each element goes

The family has three elements: the pebble (the mark), the ZimiGames tile (the product icon) and the bare Z (a material on the Zimi card back and the Ace of Spades). The system, with its conflicts and how they were settled, is in `games.md` ("The mark"); in short:

| Place | Element | Size | Clear space | Light and dark |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ZIM icon, Zimi Home, Zimi's header | Games tile | 48, 96, 256 | Half the pebble's width | As drawn |
| Shelf header | Games tile and word | 40px, 12px gap | 16px | As drawn |
| Game bar | Pebble beside the chevron | 24px | 8px | Ring in dark |
| Sheets over a bar, end screens | None | n/a | n/a | n/a |
| Card back, Ace of Spades | Bare Z | See `games.md` | See `games.md` | Same in both |
| Tab icon | `favicon.svg`: pebble on felt | 16, 32 | Full bleed | Same in both |

`build.py` also writes `favicon.svg` (the felt with the pebble alone, since the full tile is mush at 16px); `--apply` and `--check` cover it, and shell.js links it from every page.

## The icon grammar

An icon is a 1024 canvas (drawn in a 100-unit viewBox) clipped to a rounded square with rx 22.4, with a soft vignette (radial, clear to 55 percent, black at 0.42 at the edge) and the mark on top as an island near the top-left, so the art shows on every side of it. The subject is large and centred as a group, not pushed aside to make room for the mark; the pebble overlays the art. There is one light, from the upper left, and every object shades and casts its shadow from it. Colour comes from the product: ZimiGames is a green felt table (#23895a to #0f4a30 to #06261a), the Almanac is dusk over the sea. Keep the style flat-ish vector with gentle gradients and smooth edges, no outlines.

## The suits and the Ace of Spades

The four suit pips in `cards.js` (`SUIT_SHAPES`) are one family at card-maker proportions, each in a 20-unit box: the spade has a crisp cusp, symmetric lobes and a slim flared stem; the club reuses the spade's stem under three lobes round a filled centre; the heart is slimmer than a valentine, with a shallow cleft and a pointed tip; the diamond has gently bowed sides and is a little wider than a rhombus so it carries the weight of the others. Draw them together and check them side by side at corner-index size and at ace size, since one fat or lumpy suit makes the whole deck look wrong. The Ace of Spades carries the maker's mark, as real decks do: the Z alone, in its gradient, 9 units tall in the spade's belly (centre 0.3 units below the middle) with clear margins to the outline, and at tableau size the same Z in the small spade. No pebble on the Ace: we tried a stamp below the spade, the pebble inset in the belly and a subtle pebble behind the Z, and Eric chose the Z alone, as large as the margins allow. The Zimi card back carries the same Z, drawn from the same numbers.

## Deck

The French deck's jacks, queens and kings (`zimi/builtin/games/courts.js`) are Adrian Kennard's SVG playing cards: https://www.me.uk/cards/ (code at https://codeberg.org/RevK/SVG-playing-cards), released under CC0 1.0 Public Domain ("No attribution required"; the court designs follow the 19th-century Goodall & Son pattern). Taken on 2026-10-26 from the site's own generator (`makeadeck.cgi`, "Download zip file of SVG for web use", options pip=0, back=Goodall, ace=Goodall, noleft, noflip; zip sha256 386521d1f6793ab4a315ce6a5fcaac60cbbda9c5c27a4e3f60ad3eebc9bf6c40). The Codeberg repo's own LICENSE file is GPL-3.0 for the generator code; the artwork's CC0 statement is on the site's page, and what we ship is the artwork alone. Each card is six stacked layers (black, blue, red, gold, a fine and a heavy line) on a 1300 x 2000 canvas, drawn once and turned half a turn for the lower half. As on a printed deck, the corner index stands in a margin column of its own, outside the frame: the frame is a plain rectangle (one 0.5-unit rule in the ink's black, no notch, drawn along the art's edge on the inside so its outer edge is the column's edge) inset 22 units from both sides (the index's ink, at most 20.3 units from the edge, and 1.5 units of air) and as tall as the art's own proportions make it (56 x 86, centred); the art fills it to its edges, uncut, and the lower half is the same figure turned half a turn. We tried the usual 70 percent frame (the art covering a 56 x 130 or a wider box): at this index size the column leaves 56 units and the cover cut hands, a sword and a flower off at the sides, so the frame takes the art's shape instead. The number cards share that frame: the outer pips of a seven are exactly its width (22 to 78), and the pip rows run from 34 to 106 (the ink 22.4 to 117.6, within 5 units of the French frame's height and the Spanish frame's own), so a mixed hand lines up at the sides and sits in one band; the dense cards' pips are 0.92 of the sparse ones' so a ten keeps its gaps. The index is the same size and inset on every card (we kept 1.2 and 5.5: shrinking it to 1.05 would grow the figure by 5 percent and cost the phone's index a fifth of its height). The Spanish courts take the same column: the picture inside each scan's printed frame is shown 56 units wide (the broken frame itself is left out; a clean rule of ours stands in its place), so the figures are as large as the French ones; Hanafuda has no frame and no index of its own: its art and border are whole, the month is a small seal (a cream disc ringed in the month's colour) laid over the card's corner, hiding about 1.3 percent of the art and none of the border's sides, and the kind's mark (bright, animal, ribbon, chaff) stands in the white margin under it; tests/test_games_pixels.cjs checks that the frame is a plain rectangle with these insets and that no art or frame ink comes within 1.5 units of the index on any court (the Spanish courts' 10, 11 and 12 are set at 0.8 so their ink ends at 20.3 like a single digit's), that Hanafuda has no plate and its seal hides under 3 percent of the art, recolour to the card palette only (red #b3261e, black #1b1b1b, amber #e09400, and the deep blue #1d3b7a of the card backs for the lines, #2f5ca8 a step lighter for the large coats), and round the paths to whole canvas units (under 0.1 px at the largest size; 483 KB of source SVG became 182 KB of `courts.js`). The fine line layer (hatching and folds) is class `zc-hatch` and `cards.css` drops it below 100 px, so the figures read at tableau size (70 px). At the smallest size (under 41 px wide) the card shows the crown, face and shoulders from the same art. `cards.js` falls back to its own drawing when `courts.js` is not loaded; pages and tests load it between `cardbacks.js` and `cards.js`. The corner indexes (A, 2 to 10, J, Q, K, and 11 and 12 for the Spanish deck and Hanafuda's months) are the outlines of Barlow Semi Condensed ExtraBold (Copyright 2017 The Barlow Project Authors, https://github.com/jpt/barlow; SIL Open Font License 1.1, https://openfontlicense.org; the OFL permits embedding outlines provided the copyright and licence notice travel with them, so `OFL-Barlow.txt` ships beside the pages and is linked from About). `scripts/cardfont.py` cuts them out of the font file with fontTools (cap height scaled to 14 grid units, y down, ink from x 0) and writes them into the `glyphs:begin/end` block of `cards.js`; no font file ships and no font is fetched, so the ZIM draws the same offline and in Kiwix, and the SVG outlines stay crisp at any size. Source file: google/fonts `ofl/barlowsemicondensed/BarlowSemiCondensed-ExtraBold.ttf` (the first cut was Barlow Condensed Bold, replaced because it was thin at phone size; the reasons and the size rules are in games.md, "The deck's index"). We first drew our own alphabet (a slab-serif cut) and Eric rejected it: "can't we just use an existing font". The first comparison, on the real cards at 200 and 52 px: Barlow Condensed Bold and SemiBold, Oswald, Archivo (narrow and extra condensed), Barlow Bold, Inter (Bold, Display, Black, tabular figures) and Zilla Slab Bold. A real condensed cut wins because "10", "11" and "12" must fit the index column (14 units wide at the same cap height as A, J, Q and K): Barlow Condensed's "10" and "12" need a 0.93 and 0.92 squeeze (the stems stay close to the singles'), Oswald 0.95, Archivo extra condensed 0.84, narrow 0.74, Barlow 0.79, Zilla Slab 0.64 to 0.79 and Inter 0.54 to 0.64, which thins their stems visibly; Inter Black's and Zilla's A is wider than the column. The ranks' spacing is ours: a two-digit rank is its digits with 2.25 units of clear paper between the ink. Credits: the shelf footer, and the About page of every card game (`scripts/games_links.py`). The Ace of Spades rule and all pips are unchanged. We did not use Kennard's jokers (a smiley); ours stay.

The Spanish courts (`courts-es.js`, the sota, caballo and rey of the four suits) are scans of the Spanish-suited pack B. P. Grimaud printed in France in 1860, from the Bibliotheque nationale de France's Gallica (https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b10513825g and siblings), as published on Wikimedia Commons in "Category:Modern Spanish Catalan deck - Grimaud - 1860" (files "Jeu d'Aluette - Grimaud - 1860 - Jack|Knight|King of Coins|Cups|Swords|Clubs.jpg"), public domain (tagged PD-Art there). Each scan is cut to the inside of its printed frame (the frame's corners were broken where the print sets its number, which read as dashed edges), the printed number is painted out with the paper, and only the paper is lifted to the card's cream (#fdfcf8): the ink keeps the scan's own colour, a little more saturated, as a modern Fournier print is (a flat-field of the whole scan had washed it out: mean chroma of the coloured pixels 97, now 118), with nine pixels of cream round the picture; it is scaled to 250 px wide and stored as WebP (12 cards, about 25 KB each, 300 KB of WebP); the picture shows whole under the index in a clean black rule; under 41 px it sits smaller under the big index row. Reference sets compared against (looked at, never shipped): Fournier's 1878 courts (Commons, public domain) and a modern Fournier pack, Nintendo and Mushibana hanafuda, Piatnik and Belgian/Wurttemberg-pattern French courts. Rejected: Heraclio Fournier photographs (photos of modern printed cards, CC BY-SA 4.0 for the photo and no statement about the art) and the "Baraja espanola" SVGs (CC BY-SA 3.0, not compatible with GPL-3.0).

The Hanafuda faces (`hanafuda-art.js`, all 48 cards) are Louie Mantia's "SVG Hanafuda with traditional colors (red border)" on Wikimedia Commons (https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:SVG_Hanafuda_with_traditional_colors_(red_border), the "Alt2" files), licensed CC BY-SA 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/), which may be adapted into GPL-3.0 works; the share-alike travels with the adapted art, and the credit is in the shelf footer and on the Koi-Koi About page. Changes: each card's paper backing and clip removed (a nested svg clips instead), the paths rounded to whole canvas units (976 x 1600; 1.6 MB of source SVG became 793 KB, almost all of it the black line art, which needs the resolution), whites, blacks, reds and ambers set to the card palette, the three black borders made brown-red like the rest. The month hues (purple, pink, cyan, green, pale gold) are kept: they are what tells the months apart. Both files are loaded only by the page that deals them (truco, koikoi), between `courts.js` and `cards.js`; `cards.js` falls back to its own drawings without them.

## The physics rules we learned the hard way

ZimiGames is one scene with one camera and one light, and every object follows from them. The camera is a true pinhole 700 mm from the point it looks at, at 58 degrees elevation, looking along +y; each object is projected through the same function, never drawn by eye. The light is 58 degrees up, from the left and a little behind, so shadows fall to the right and slightly toward the viewer, longer for taller things; shadows are projected from the real geometry, sharp at contact and softer with height. The derivation is in the comments at the top of the games block in `build.py`.

A die is right-handed (a Western die: 1, 2 and 3 meet counter-clockwise at a corner), opposite faces sum to 7, and the code asserts both; the three visible faces must be mutually adjacent. Pips are circles on their face planes, projected through the camera, so the side pips foreshorten with the face and are never smeared blobs; the top 5 is a true quincunx on that face's grid. The die is one rounded cube tessellated through its flat faces and rounded rim, each patch shaded from its own normal, not chamfers with gradient strips, which look lumpy. A card lies flat in the table plane. A joystick's shaft axis runs through the centre of the ball, the stick stands up out of a washer and boot, the shaft is one chrome cylinder with one smooth highlight (no hard banding), and the stick casts one shadow, a single chain of rings from foot to ball that softens with height and matches the light. The ball's highlight sits on the half vector between the light and the camera. Do not move the card or the die when fixing the stick: the fit scale is frozen in `build.py` on purpose.

## The Almanac rules

The Almanac icon is a real chart, so it has to be astronomically right. It is the Sun's altitude through one real day in San Francisco (37.77 N, 122.42 W), the solstice arcs, the Moon's path, and one line through the Moon and the Sun at the golden hour (the Sun 6 degrees up). The positions come from a real ephemeris, not eyeballing: `astro.py` is Schlyter's low-precision Sun and Moon (good to about a tenth of a degree, plenty for an icon), with the Moon's topocentric parallax applied. The Moon's lit side points at the Sun, so `bright_limb_angle` builds the Moon-to-Sun direction in a local East, North, Up frame (right-handed), projects it into the Moon's tangent plane and measures it against local up; it is the on-screen direction, not the position angle from north.

The sea carries the Sun's glitter, computed from Cox and Munk (1954): facet slopes are Gaussian with variance 0.003 + 0.00512 W for wind W m/s (5 here), radiance goes as the slope density over 4 cos^4(tilt) cos(view zenith), so it is brightest in a thin band at the horizon, then a column of glints that fades and widens downward. The Sun is drawn as an extended source, averaged over its disc as drawn (`glitter.glints_ext`), not a point; the drawn Sun is the one enlargement, since at true size it would be a quarter of a unit across. The date is locked on 23 March 2026 (`DAY`, override only with `ALM_DAY` to explore): a waxing crescent, 31 percent lit, high in the evening sky, near the equinox, with a Moon that reads at small sizes. We checked birthdays (20 April 2023, a new Moon that sits on the Sun as a dark disc; 18 April 2025, a waning gibbous that is under the sea at sunset and on top of the Sun at sunrise); each is accurate and each reads worse at icon size, so the icon stays on March. Do not change the art or the date.

## Backs

The twelve card backs live in `zimi/builtin/games/cardbacks.js`, each drawn by code in the card's 92 x 132 art box (centre 46,66), pure SVG, no bitmaps; `cards.js` takes `BACKS`, `backMarkup` and the shared gradients from it, and the Settings picker lists every back by name. The rule for a patterned back: a band of ground colour with two rules (outer at inset 3.5, hairline at 7.5), then a field of whole cells starting and ending on a cell line, centred, so the pattern meets the band at its inner edge and never leaves a sliver; symmetric across both axes and the same turned 180 degrees (a half that points up is the other half turned a half turn about the centre); the cell math is a comment above each back. Cells are 5 to 14 units, so at tableau size (about 40px wide, 0.4px a unit) they read as texture, not a moire. Only the Zimi back carries the mark; it is also the only one with the Z, centred in a cartouche the lattice stops on. The scenes (night, sea, castle) are pictures: they get the band and a clean panel and are not symmetric. The animations are the classes in `cards.css`.

The set: Zimi (black and amber lattice, the Z), Classic (royal blue diamond lattice, ivory and red medallion), Night sky (today's Moon), The sea, Robot (graph paper), Castle, Rosette (Victorian guilloche in oxblood and gold), Art Deco (brass scales on bottle green, mirrored at the seam), Waves (seigaiha in vermilion with the rising sun on the seam), Zellige (turquoise, ivory and ochre eight-pointed stars in a chessboard), Star chart (the north celestial pole with the real Big Dipper, Ursa Minor and Cassiopeia from their right ascension and declination, one unit a degree) and Contours (a survey sheet with a neat line of alternating segments). No two share a colour or a density.

## The review loop

Nothing goes to Eric before you have looked at it yourself. Render at 1024, then 96, 48 and 32 (`--review` makes the board), crop at 4x on the die, the stick, the Z and every edge, and look at each crop with your own eyes; counting things in the DOM or trusting the maths is not looking. Check it on the real surfaces too: the back button at 24 px, a sheet heading at 22 px and the shelf header at 390 px in WebKit, light and dark. Then get a second pair of eyes (a reviewer agent briefed with the camera and light numbers and asked for every physical inconsistency), fix what is real, and look again. Eric reviews on his phone, where he zooms in, so what survives 4x zoom on a phone screenshot is the bar. Iterate on one flaw at a time, keep the parts he approved exactly where they are, and never use em dashes in anything you write about it.

## A note on the font's licence

SF Compact is Apple's font, and its licence covers mockups for Apple platforms. We ship its Z as outlines, in an icon that already ships (`zimi/assets/icon.png` carries the same glyph), and the generator reads the font only to take that outline. This is noted here so it is on record; nothing has been changed because of it.
