# Apps

Six views Zimi owns over the ZIMs in your library. Each is a page inside the reader with the library's search box on top, and each reads its ZIMs' own indexes rather than a copy, so nothing is rebuilt and nothing goes stale.

## How it works

The home page shows an **Apps** row above the sources when the library holds something an app can read, and on a fresh install the row still shows: each tile then says what it needs and opens the matching catalog category (or, for Reddot, the Create page).

The library's order and its list or grid view sit on the **Apps** heading, above the row, and govern the whole page, apps included: alphabetical puts the apps by name, **Recently added** by the newest ZIM that arrived in each, **Recently updated** by the newest ZIM that arrived or was updated in each, **Most articles** by the articles in all of an app's ZIMs together. The **Apps** heading opens the Apps page: each app with the ZIMs inside it, in the same order; an app's name opens the app, a ZIM's card opens the ZIM.

- **Maps** opens the last map you visited at the place you left it. A StreetZim map nobody has been on yet opens over its settlements rather than the middle of its region's box (Hawaii's box is mostly ocean). The box finds a place on the open map (StreetZim's own place index; Kiwix maps' place pages). The picker under the pin lists the installed maps that cover the spot on screen first, then the catalog's maps of here and of the whole world, then the rest folded away, then **Where I am** (asked for when tapped, never before) and **All maps in the catalog**. Switching maps keeps the position and zoom when the other map covers it. Random rolls a place; history and bookmarks record places. The pin opens **Places and maps**: **Save this place** and the places you keep come first (see [saving](saving.md)), then the maps.
- **ZimiTube** is one feed across every video ZIM (TED, YouTube, and the ones Zimi makes). Browsing is a shelf per source; a source, a query, or a library with one source is a list with an order of its own (Top, Title, Newest, Longest). A card plays in ZimiTube's own player with the page's subtitles, the rest of the list up next, and autoplay into it. Leave the player with a video playing and it docks in a corner while you browse; tap it to come back. Theater mode gives the stage the whole width, and Picture in picture appears where the browser has it. TED's videos are WebM, which iPhones cannot decode; on an iPhone or iPad ZimiTube plays them through the decoder the TED ZIM ships (ogv.js) from the start, and the talk's original page, read through Zimi, does the same. The same talk carried by two ZIMs is one card. The original page is one tap away. **Like**, **Save** and **Lists** sit under a video, and the home starts with **Continue watching**, **Saved**, **Liked** and your lists (see [saving](saving.md)).
- **ZimiExchange** is every Stack Exchange site in the library as one place: a shelf per site of its most voted questions with its top tags, a site or a tag as a paged list, and a question with its answers, the accepted one first. The box searches question titles across the installed sites. **Like**, **Save** and **Lists** sit under a question's title, and **Saved** among the chips lists what you kept.
- **Reddot** reads subreddit ZIMs (ArcticZim's, and the ones Zimi makes): a shelf per subreddit (a library with one subreddit opens on that subreddit's list), a subreddit as a list (Top, New), a post with its comment tree. Follow subreddits with the star on their shelves and **Home** lists their top posts in one place; followed shelves come first. What you follow is kept in your browser. **Like**, **Save** and **Lists** sit under a post's title, and **Saved** among the chips lists what you kept. To make one, paste a subreddit's reddit.com address into Create under Web page; see [making ZIMs](making-zims.md).
- **Zimipedia** is every wiki in the library (Wikipedia in each language, its sister projects, and the MediaWiki wikis beyond them), one language at a time. **Today** is its front door: the day's article (its picture at its own shape, a third of the screen at most), **On this day**, the **Picture of the day** (whole, never cropped; a tap shows it large, its caption opens its article), **Did you know**, **Continue reading** (the articles you are part way through, each opening at the section you were in) and **Recent trails**. The box searches the language's wikis; the pills narrow it and one tap reaches every language. An article opens in **Zimipedia's reader**: Reader View laid out as an encyclopedia, with Bookshelf's reading settings (**Aa**: theme, font, size, line spacing, margins), the lead image, contents that follow you (a rail beside the text on a wide screen, a sheet from the bar on a phone), the infobox beside the text (folded as **Quick facts** on a phone), and a citation's note in a card where you tapped it. Its bar steps aside with Zimi's header while you read.
  - **Languages** lists only the installed languages that have the article, matched by its Wikidata ID, and opens it at the same section: the section of the same name, else the one at the same place in the article. On a wide screen a language can open beside the article instead. Simple English is there as a reading level. A mini build that carries no ID borrows it from a fuller build of its language holding the same page; a mini says it is one (the introduction and the key facts, without references) and links to the full article when one is installed. The button is not shown when it cannot work.
  - **In the library**, under the lead: what the other wikis of the language hold on the topic (the Wiktionary entry, the Wikivoyage guide, Wikiquote, Wikibooks, Wikisource) and a map of the place when an installed map covers it.
  - **Diving in**: a link shows a card first (the article's first sentence and picture); **Read**, or a second tap, follows it. The articles you came through sit over the title as a trail, a tap going back along it, and **Read next** ends an article with the ones its lead links to.
  - The bookmark in the bar saves the article to Zimipedia's reading lists: Liked, any list, or a new one.
  - A wiki's article opened from anywhere else reads in Zimipedia's reader when Reader View is on.
- **Bookshelf** is every book in the library as one shelf: Project Gutenberg's, the documents in Kiwix's document libraries and in Zimi's own folders, LibreTexts textbooks, Wikisource works, Wikibooks, and ZIMs that are one book. Its front has **Continue reading**, **Saved** (the books you keep, with **Save** on a book's page), **Most read** and **Newest to Project Gutenberg** (Gutenberg's own measures, shown when its books are there; a shelf without them opens on **All books**), **Sources** (each ZIM the books come from, with how many; a tap lists its books), the subjects and the eras, then tabs for **Authors** (A to Z, most books, and most read where Gutenberg is, with their years), **Subjects** (the Library of Congress class Gutenberg files each book under, when there are Gutenberg books), **Eras** (a bar per hundred years, placed by when the author lived, since the ZIM records no first publication date) and **Languages**. A book's page has its cover (one set in type when the ZIM has no picture), its author, what its source says of it, its source, language, subject, format (a PDF or an EPUB), chapters, translator, publisher, when it was published, era and the day it came to Gutenberg, **Read** and the EPUB; on a phone **Read** comes before those facts. A shelf whose ZIMs hold no book it can open names them. The box searches titles and authors. A book opens in the book reader, and Zimi's own header steps aside for it. Its header has the way back, the title and author, **Contents** (the book's chapters; a tap goes there) and **Aa** (reading settings); its footer has the chapter you are in, the pages left in it, how far through the book you are, a slider to go anywhere and a step to the chapter before or after. Both step aside while you read and come back on a tap in the middle of the page, or, scrolling, on a scroll back up. Read by **Pages** (a tap at either side or a swipe turns the page; a right-to-left book turns the other way; on a wide screen two pages sit side by side; the arrow keys and the wheel turn them too) or by **Scroll**; pages are the first choice on a touch screen, scrolling with a mouse. **Aa** sets the theme (Auto follows the system, Light, Sepia, Dark), the font (serif or sans), the text size, the line spacing, the margins and the layout, remembered in this browser; the theme and font are Reader View's, so articles follow them too. The reader keeps your place in each book to the character, so it holds through a change of type, a turn of the phone, a change between pages and scrolling, and reopening; the shelf's Continue reading shows how far you are, and, signed in, a book read on one device continues on another. Subjects, eras and years are read from each book's catalog record in the background the first time a ZIM is seen; the shelf opens at once and they appear when that is done.

**Getting around.** The app's icon in the breadcrumb is the way to its front page. The arrow in Zimi's header is the only back there is, and it works inside an app as it does in an article: it appears once you are inside (a video, a question, a post, a list) and steps back to where you were; a list (a subreddit, a tag, a search) steps to the app's home; at the home there is no arrow, as on a ZIM's front page. **Open the original page** is a step too: the arrow returns to the video, the question or the post. A shared link to one of those has no home beneath it, so the arrow makes one in place. The dice stay inside the app: a video, a question or a post by chance. Back from a thing lands where you were in its list. On a phone, Zimi's header slides away as you scroll down into an app's page (and the Almanac) and comes back as you scroll up; a video playing with the phone turned sideways fills the screen until it is paused. In Reddot, tapping a comment's line folds it with its replies. A shared link to a video, a question or a post is a query (`/?tube=…`, `/?exchange=…`, `/?reddot=…`), which survives a sign-in on the way in.

**Saved and history.** A video, a question or a post open in an app can be saved with the same button as an article, and History remembers it the same way; both show it under the app's own name and mark, and opening it there opens it in the app. The Saved panel opens from an app on that app's own saved things. See [Saving](saving.md).

**One thing once.** When two ZIMs carry the same thing, an app shows it once, from the newest build (then the fullest): last month's file still beside this month's, a nopic beside a maxi of one site, the same region from the same map source, a subreddit in two bundles. The library itself lists every file; only the apps' views are deduplicated.

**Choosing apps.** Each app can be offered or not. `ZIMI_APPS=0` hides them all for the whole server and `ZIMI_APPS=maps,tube` keeps only those (the names are `maps`, `tube`, `exchange`, `reddot`, `books` and `wiki`); the **Apps** tiles at the top of Preferences do the same without a restart, and each account can untick apps for itself under its own settings. The pages and endpoints stay reachable by address for anyone allowed to read the ZIMs behind them.

## Configure

| Setting | Where | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ZIMI_APPS` | environment | `0` offers no app to anyone; a comma list of `maps`, `tube`, `exchange`, `reddot`, `books`, `wiki` offers only those. Overrides the saved choice. |
| Apps | Preferences, first section | The server's choice: the six app tiles, lit when offered, with All and None; each tile says what the library holds for it. Saved with the other server preferences. |
| Apps on my home page | account settings | One account's own choice among the apps the server offers, kept with its bookmarks and history. |

## Troubleshoot

- **Zimipedia's reader shows no languages button on an article**: this copy of the article carries no Wikidata ID and no fuller build of its language is installed to lend one, or no other installed language has it. A big Wikipedia's IDs are learned as its articles are read, so a language can appear on a later visit.
- **A citation says the copy does not carry it**: the ZIM is a mini build (the introduction and the key facts), made without its references. A nopic or maxi build of the same wiki has them.
- **A video ZIM is missing from ZimiTube** — Zimi reads ted2zim, youtube2zim and its own index shapes. Another scraper's ZIM opens as a source but not in ZimiTube. Restart after installing one made by an older version so its kind is read again.
- **A talk I can see in the ZIM is not in ZimiTube** — its file is not in the ZIM (the scraper wrote the page and skipped a download that failed), so the feed leaves it out. Its page still opens as an article and says so.
- **A book collection is missing from Bookshelf**: Bookshelf reads Gutenberg, the document libraries, LibreTexts, Wikisource, Wikibooks, ZIMs that are one book and ZIMs Zimi makes from a folder of documents; the first time it sees a ZIM it reads its books in the background, and a big one takes a few minutes. Restart after installing one made by an older version so its kind is read again.
- **A book shows no Contents list, or one entry for the whole book**: the chapters are the book's own headings; a book without them reads as one run. Its pages still turn and its place is still kept.
- **Bookshelf has no Eras or Subjects yet**: they come from reading every book's record once, in the background; the full English Gutenberg takes a few minutes. The note on the shelf goes away when it is done.
- **A site shows twice in ZimiExchange** — two ZIMs with different titles are two sites. Two builds with one title collapse to the newest.
- **"This video cannot be played here."** — the file is in the ZIM but this browser cannot decode it. When a page names a WebM and the ZIM also carries an MP4 of the same talk, the MP4 is played instead; Safari gets the ZIM's own decoder for a WebM, as an iPhone does. The link under the message opens the video's own page, which may carry a decoder of its own (TED's pages do). A ZIM Zimi makes from a video site takes H.264 first for this reason; one made before 1.10 may carry AV1, which no iPhone before the 15 Pro decodes.
- **"This video isn't in this ZIM."** — the video's page is there but its file never was: the scraper wrote the page and skipped a download that failed (the CRISPR talk in the 2023 TED technology ZIM). Nothing on your side is wrong; a newer build of the ZIM may carry it.
- **A place I can see on a Kiwix map is not in the search** — Kiwix's maps carry one place page per name, so "Danville" finds one Danville and a town that shares its name with a bigger one has no page of its own. A StreetZim map of the region carries every place and address.
- **The map picker does not offer a map of here** — the catalog's regions are approximate boxes; the row **All maps in the catalog** lists every one.
