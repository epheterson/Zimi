Where the files in this folder come from (the Almanac's Earth view, almanac-earth.js).

earth-day-v1.webp
  NASA Earth Observatory, Blue Marble: Next Generation, July 2004, topography
  (world.topo.200407.3x5400x2700.jpg), Reto Stockli, NASA Goddard Space Flight
  Center. Public domain (NASA imagery). Resized to 2048x1024, WebP quality 75.
  https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/blue-marble-next-generation/

earth-night-v1.webp
  NASA Earth Observatory, Black Marble 2016 (BlackMarble_2016_01deg.jpg),
  Joshua Stevens / Miguel Roman, NASA. Public domain. Greyscale, 2048x1024,
  WebP quality 75.
  https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/features/NightLights

moon-v1.webp
  NASA Scientific Visualization Studio, CGI Moon Kit (lroc_color_poles_1k.jpg),
  from the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera. Public domain. Greyscale,
  1024x512, WebP quality 75. Also the map every 2D Moon is drawn from (the
  Almanac hero, the Today card, the sky scene; app.js _MOON_MAP_URL).
  https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720

moon-4k-v1.webp
  NASA Scientific Visualization Studio, CGI Moon Kit (lroc_color_poles_4k.tif),
  from the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera's Wide Angle Camera mosaic.
  Public domain. Greyscale, 4096x2048, WebP quality 75 (cwebp 1.6.0). Fetched
  after the first paint and drawn wherever a Moon is wide enough to show it
  (the hero, the 3D view; app.js _MOON_MAP_HI_URL). Credited with the other
  NASA images in the 3D view ("Images: NASA").
  https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720

three-r186.min.js (three-LICENSE.txt)
  three.js r186, MIT License. Tree-shaken and minified; the build line is in
  the file's first comment.

satellites-all-snapshot.json.gz (zimi/assets), the satellites' elements
  CelesTrak (celestrak.org) GP data for the active group and its SATCAT (owner, launch date, radar cross-section),
  fetched when the release was cut and, where the Satellite data setting allows, again at runtime. Credited in the
  3D view as CelesTrak.

satellite-7.1.0.min.js (satellite.js-LICENSE.txt)
  satellite.js 7.1.0, MIT License: SGP4/SDP4 only. The build line is in the
  file's first comment.

stars-v1.bin
  Yale Bright Star Catalogue, 5th revised edition (BSC5), Hoffleit and Warren,
  1991 (Astronomical Data Center catalogue V/50). Public domain. 9,096 stars to
  visual magnitude 7.96 with a J2000 position: right ascension and declination,
  V magnitude and B-V, packed to 6 bytes a star (54,580 bytes) by
  scripts/build_star_catalog.py, which documents the format. Drawn as the 3D
  view's star sphere (almanac-earth.js _aeStarField), fetched the first time
  that view opens.
  http://tdc-www.harvard.edu/catalogs/bsc5.html

milkyway-v1.webp
  ESO / S. Brunier, "The Milky Way panorama" (eso0932a), a photographic
  all-sky mosaic in galactic coordinates. CC BY 4.0: credit ESO/S. Brunier,
  https://www.eso.org/public/images/eso0932a/ . Changed by Zimi: the point
  stars removed (a grey opening over a 5 px window, so the dust lanes stay and
  the catalogue stars in stars-v1.bin are not drawn twice), a faint noise
  floor subtracted, resized to 2048x1024, WebP quality 90. Longitude runs to
  the left from the centre (l = 0 in the middle), latitude +90 at the top;
  almanac-skygl.js _sgGalUV is that map. Drawn behind the stars of the live
  sky (almanac-sky.js). Credited in THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md.

constellations-v1.json
  The 88 IAU constellations' figures and label places, packed by
  scripts/build_constellations.py from d3-celestial's constellations.lines.json
  and constellations.json (Olaf Frohn, BSD 3-Clause, github.com/ofrohn/d3-celestial;
  the lines follow the IAU's constellation pages with the author's
  modifications). Licence text: d3-celestial-LICENSE.txt. Stellarium's
  constellation data is GPL and is not used.
  Drawn by the live sky (almanac-sky.js _skyPaintConstellations), 15 KB.

Moon and Earth: the Earth view's maps above (moon-v1.webp, earth-day-v1.webp,
earth-night-v1.webp). Saturn's rings are drawn from their measured radial
structure (Cassini Division 117,500-122,200 km, Encke Gap near 133,500 km).

landmarks-v1.json
  Named places on every body the 3D view draws, for its labels, cards and Fly
  there, built by scripts/build_landmarks.py: one list per body (Moon, Mars,
  Venus, Titan, Mercury, Earth, Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto, Pluto, Ceres),
  each row [name, lat, lon, kind, Wikidata Q-ID, size km, source], the last an
  index into the file's own "sources" list. Longitudes are east, -180..180, the
  frame every map in this folder is drawn in. The features are the IAU
  Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature (USGS Astrogeology Science Center, public
  domain; the largest of each kind, craters by size per body, a few named by
  hand), each with its Q-ID by Gazetteer ID (P2824; Wikidata is CC0). The
  landing sites are at the landers' published coordinates: 25 on the Moon (NASA
  NSSDC and the LRO camera team, CNSA, ISRO, JAXA), 10 on Mars (NASA/JPL, CNSA),
  8 on Venus (Venera and Vega, NASA NSSDC) and Huygens on Titan (ESA/NASA), each
  with the Q-ID of its English Wikipedia article. Earth's 31 places (the highest
  peaks, a few wonders, launch sites) are at their published coordinates, each
  checked against Wikidata's (CC0). Each row also has a fame (the number of Wikipedia editions with an article on it, from Wikidata, plus a bonus for the body's best-known places) that orders the names. 139 KB, fetched the first time a body with landmarks
  is near.
  https://planetarynames.wr.usgs.gov/

Almanac HD (zimi_almanac_maxi_<month>.zim, not in this folder: the HD flavour of the
Almanac, built on each release from a pinned tile source and fetched once on an
admin's yes; zimi/almanachd.py, scripts/build_almanac_maxi.py)
  The Earth and the Moon as 512 px tile pyramids, built by
  scripts/build_almanac_hd.py, all public domain:
  - Earth by day: NASA Blue Marble: Next Generation (Reto Stockli, NASA Earth
    Observatory), through NASA GIBS (500 m set), to 610 m a pixel.
  - Earth by night: NASA Black Marble 2016 (VIIRS), through NASA GIBS.
  - Moon: the CGI Moon Kit's LRO colour mosaic at 16k (above), and the
    Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter's elevation at 64 pixels a degree
    (ldem_64_uint.tif) as surface normals, so the Moon is lit by its relief.
    https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720

saturn-v1.webp, uranus-v1.webp, neptune-v1.webp
  Solar System Scope (INOVE), 2k_saturn.jpg, 2k_uranus.jpg, 2k_neptune.jpg,
  CC BY 4.0, https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/ . Resized to 1024x512,
  WebP quality 80. Credited on the view while one of them is drawn

space-tracks-v1.json, scripts/build_space_tracks.py, scripts/fit_moon_orbits.py
  Spacecraft and outer-planet state vectors from JPL Horizons (public), thinned
  for Hermite interpolation; the big moons' mean circular orbits fitted to it.

asteroids-v1.bin (scripts/build_asteroids.py)
  The main belt's asteroids brighter than H 14 (17,992), osculating elements
  from NASA/JPL's Small-Body Database (SBDB Query API), brought to one epoch
  and quantized to 16 bits, 216 KB. Moved by Kepler's laws in the view.

iss-v1.bin (scripts/build_craft_models.py)
  The International Space Station: NASA 3D Resources' "International Space
  Station (ISS) (B)", credit NASA/Michael D. Carbajal; the collection's terms:
  "These assets are free and without copyright". No textures (no logo).
  Simplified from 174,000 triangles to 31,000 and quantized, 331 KB.

almanac-sun.js
  Code, not data: the Sun's prominences, flares and finer surface, made up by
  the Sun's own rules (like the sunspots) and labelled illustrative in the view.
  Rotation: IAU WGCCRE 2015 (Carrington rate), differential rotation from
  Snodgrass and Ulrich 1990.
  - Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn at 8192 px: Solar System Scope's
    8k maps, CC BY 4.0, credited on the view while drawn.

Map overlay on the 3D Earth (almanac-maps.js)
  - Roads and place names read at run time from the installed Kiwix/StreetZim
    map ZIM's vector tiles (OpenMapTiles schema). Map data (c) OpenStreetMap
    contributors, ODbL; credited on the view while drawn. Nothing is shipped.
craft-site-<model>-v1.bin, sites-v1.json (scripts/build_site_models.py)
  The landers, rovers, flags and the Saturn V the 3D view stands on the Moon and
  the planets (almanac-sites.js), in the ZCRF format of the probes above.
  From NASA 3D Resources ("These assets are free and without copyright"):
  lm: "Apollo Lunar Module" (descent stage only: it is what stayed), no credit listed
  perseverance-body, perseverance-wheel: "Mars 2020 Perseverance Rover", NASA/JPL-Caltech;
    also the Curiosity site's rover (the same family; the wheels split off to turn)
  ingenuity: "Ingenuity Mars Helicopter", NASA/JPL-Caltech
  mer: "Mars Exploration Rover - Opportunity (MER-B)" for Spirit and Opportunity, NASA/JPL-Caltech
  insight: "InSight Cruise Lander (arm deployed)", NASA/JPL-Caltech
  saturn5: "Saturn V", no credit listed
  Written for Zimi from the craft's published dimensions (boxes, cylinders, spheres;
  no third-party geometry): the Lunar Roving Vehicle, Lunokhod, the Chang'e lander,
  Yutu, Zhurong, Viking, Pathfinder, Sojourner, Phoenix, Huygens, Venera,
  Surveyor, the US and Chinese flags, Launch Complex 39A and its tower.
  sites-v1.json: where each model stands at a landing the landmarks file names,
  the landing's instant (NASA mission pages, NSSDC, CNSA) and the rovers' wheels.
  The Saturn V's flight (almanac-sites-launch.js) is computed from thrust, mass and
  burn rate (NASA Saturn V flight manual, SA-506); the pitch program is fitted to
  the Apollo 11 flight report's altitude, range and speed at first-stage cut-off.

traverses-v1.json (scripts/build_traverses.py)
  Curiosity's and Perseverance's drives, a point per sol where the route turns:
  NASA/JPL's MMGIS waypoint files (mars.nasa.gov/mmgis-maps/MSL, /M20), public
  domain, credit "NASA/JPL-Caltech/MSL and Mars 2020 teams". 45 KB.
